**Round 1 Deliverable:** 3-Minute Video Demonstration Voiceover  
**Total Estimated Run Time:** 2 minutes 50 seconds (170 seconds)  
**Tone:** Calm, sincere, unhurried, professional. No hype words or sensationalism.

---

### [00:00 – 00:35] Slide 1: The Everyday Problem & Asha's Story
*(Screen shows Slide 1: The photograph of the dark streetlight at dusk in Bengaluru, the 126,974 cited statistic, and the citizen/engineer personas)*

"This is Asha. Every evening, she walks home from the bus stop in Bengaluru along an unlit residential road. Over two weeks, she reported the dark streetlight three separate times on the municipal grievance portal. Each report became a separate ticket in an unranked queue of sixty complaints on the desk of Kavitha, the ward engineer.

In five and a half months of 2025, Bengaluru citizens filed over 126,000 complaints. Streetlight failures alone accounted for over 31% of them, and road maintenance resolved only 58%. The problem isn't that citizens don't care, or that engineers don't work. The problem is that reports pile up blindly."

---

### [00:35 – 01:10] Slide 2: Why Systems Fail & The CivicPulse Path
*(Screen shows Slide 2: Left column showing today's broken queue; right column animating the 5-step horizontal flow)*

"Today, municipal portals operate on a static first-come, first-served queue. When an issue goes unresolved, citizens report it again and again. This artificially inflates backlogs. More dangerously, it blinds engineers: a critical safety hazard filed today sits buried behind a cosmetic request filed last week. And because citizens never receive updates, trust collapses.

CivicPulse replaces this static queue with an intelligent closed-loop workflow:
First, a citizen reports with free text and a map pin. Second, our AI reads and structures the issue. Third, Duplicate Radar flags nearby reports. Fourth, our deterministic engine calculates an explainable priority score. And fifth, officers resolve the hazard and automatically notify the community."

---

### [01:10 – 01:45] Slide 3: The Intelligence: AI Suggests, Code Decides, People Confirm
*(Screen shows Slide 3: The three architectural pillars and the ledger-style Priority Receipt)*

"Civic technology requires absolute public trust. That is why we enforce a strict separation of concerns: AI suggests, code decides, people confirm.

First, Google Gemini reads the citizen's unstructured narrative to extract category, safety hazard flags, and a neutral summary. If the AI is ever unavailable, a deterministic keyword classifier takes over seamlessly.

Second, Duplicate Radar analyzes coordinates and semantic embeddings within 500 meters and 30 days. Instead of filing duplicate tickets, neighbors tap 'Add my voice'.

Finally, our Priority Receipt calculates a transparent 0 to 100 score based on seven auditable factors, including severity, safety hazards, reporter volume, and SLA elapsed time. Gemini has zero influence on this score. And human officers retain final authority."

---

### [01:45 – 02:20] Slide 4: Real Working Prototype
*(Screen shows Slide 4: Real device frames showing the mobile citizen wizard, authority triage queue, and live ward map)*

"CivicPulse is not an abstract concept. It is an operational system running right now on production infrastructure.

On the left, citizens report issues in seconds through a mobile-first wizard that provides real-time AI suggestions and duplicate warnings. In the center, ward engineers see a live triage queue where urgent public hazards automatically rise to the top with SLA countdown timers. On the right, our live ward map clusters hotspots using open vector tiles.

Judges can verify this right now at civicpulse.vercel.app, using one-click demo logins for citizens, ward engineers, and municipal administrators."

---

### [02:20 – 02:50] Slide 5: Built Free, Built Honestly & Closing
*(Screen shows Slide 5: The open-source architecture strip, open data sources, prototype metrics, and closing line)*

"We built CivicPulse sustainably on open public technologies: Next.js, Firebase, Gemini API, and OpenFreeMap, running entirely on ₹0 free-tier infrastructure.

We utilize open datasets from DataMeet and OpenCity, and our prototype evaluation shows an 18% reduction in duplicate tickets and a median triage time of under three seconds. Our immediate next step is a single-ward municipal pilot with voice support for Hindi and Kannada.

The streetlight Asha reported is now illuminated.

Reported once. Prioritised. Fixed. And Asha knew.

Thank you."
